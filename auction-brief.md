Bidding on Copart involves two main phases: **Preliminary Bidding (Pre-bidding)** and the **Live Virtual Auction (VB3)**. 

Here is a step-by-step breakdown of how the live auction and bidding system work.

---

### 1. The Two Bidding Stages

#### Stage A: Preliminary Bidding (Pre-Bidding)
* Before the live auction begins, you can submit bids on any vehicle up until **one hour** before the live sale starts (or until the vehicle enters the live auction block).
* You enter the **maximum amount** you are willing to pay. 
* Copart’s automated system will bid on your behalf incrementally, only going as high as necessary to keep you as the high bidder, up to your maximum.
* If nobody outbids your pre-bid during the live auction, you win the vehicle.

#### Stage B: Live Virtual Auction (VB3)
* When the auction starts, vehicles are auctioned off in sequence within designated "lanes" (Lane A, Lane B, etc.).
* Each vehicle is typically on the block for only **60 to 90 seconds**, moving very quickly.

---

### 2. How the Live Auction Screen Works

When you enter a live auction room, you will see an interactive dashboard:

1. **The Dynamic Countdown Timer:**
   * An animated circular clock counts down (from green to yellow to red).
   * **Every time a new bid is placed, the timer resets** to give other bidders a few seconds to react.
   * When the timer runs out without any new bids, the vehicle is hammered sold.

2. **Placing a Bid:**
   * **Incremental Bid:** Click the large bid button showing the next minimum increment (e.g., if the bid is $1,200, the button might say "Bid $1,300").
   * **Monster Bid:** You can manually type in a much higher bid instead of following the standard increment. This allows you to jump ahead and deter competitors.

3. **Audio/Visual Prompts:**
   * The system will announce the current bid amount and flash notifications stating whether you are the current high bidder or have been outbid.

---

### 3. Understanding Sale Statuses (Crucial)

Even if you are the highest bidder when the clock expires, whether you immediately get the vehicle depends on the **Sale Status**:

* **Pure Sale:** There is no reserve price. The highest bidder wins the vehicle outright as soon as the auction ends.
* **On Approval:** The seller (often an insurance company or bank) has reserved the right to accept, reject, or counter your winning bid. They usually have until 8:00 PM (local yard time) the next business day to decide.
* **Minimum Bid (Reserve):** The seller has set a hidden minimum price. If the auction ends below that minimum, the status switches to "On Approval" and the seller can choose to negotiate or decline.

---

### 4. What You Need Before You Can Bid

* **Membership Tier:** 
  * **Basic:** You must deposit 10% of your intended bid (e.g., a $400 deposit gives you $4,000 in buying power).
  * **Premier:** Requires a one-time fully refundable deposit and higher annual fee, but provides virtually unlimited bidding power.
* **Licensing Requirements:**
  * Some states or countries restrict public buyers from purchasing salvage titles directly. In those jurisdictions, you may need a business license or have to bid through a registered **Copart Broker**.

---

### 5. What Happens After Winning

1. **Buyer Fees:** The hammer price is not the total price. Copart adds buyer fees, internet bidding fees, and gate/loading fees (which increase with the vehicle's price).
2. **Payment Window:** You typically have **3 business days** (including the day of sale) to pay in full via wire transfer, cashier's check, or secure electronic transfer to avoid late fees ($50+).
3. **Storage & Pick-Up:** You usually have **2 to 3 days of complimentary storage** to haul the vehicle off the lot before daily storage fees begin accruing. Most buyers arrange transport using Copart Delivery or a third-party auto transporter.

Copart’s membership tiers are designed to protect the auction against non-paying
bidders. To ensure you will actually pay for a car if you win, Copart controls
your "Buying Power."

Here is a detailed breakdown of what that means, how each tier works, and how to
choose the right one.

What is "Buying Power"?

Buying Power is the maximum dollar amount you are allowed to bid across all
active auctions at any given moment. It is not credit or free money; it is
simply your bidding ceiling.

1. Basic Membership (Best for Casual / One-Time Buyers)

  - Annual Fee: ~$99/year.
  - How the 10% Deposit Works:
      - For every dollar you want to bid, you must hold 10% in cash as a
        security deposit in your Copart account.
      - Example:
          - If you want to bid up to 2,500**, you must deposit **250.
          - If you want to bid up to 7,000**, you must deposit **700.
  - Key Rules & Limitations:
      - One vehicle at a time: Basic members are generally restricted to bidding
        on one vehicle at a time.
      - Your cash is tied up while bidding: Once you place a preliminary bid or
        enter a live auction, that deposit is locked until the auction ends. If
        you don't win, the deposit is freed up, and you can either request a
        refund back to your card or keep it there for your next bid.
      - If you win: Your deposit does not automatically pay for the vehicle. You
        must pay the full purchase price via wire transfer or cashier’s check.
        Once you pay and pick up the car, you can request your deposit back.

2. Premier Membership (Best for Dealers, Exporters & Frequent Buyers)

  - Annual Fee: ~$249/year.
  - The Deposit: Requires a flat, one-time refundable deposit of $400.
  - What "Virtually Unlimited" Means:
      - Instead of calculating 10% for every single car, that single
        400 deposit unlocks substantial daily bidding power (typically **100,000+**
        per day).
      - You can bid on multiple vehicles simultaneously across different lanes
        without having to transfer more money beforehand.
  - Other Perks:
      - Priority customer support and dedicated phone queues.
      - More efficient for bidding on higher-value vehicles (e.g., to bid
        $40,000 on a Basic plan, you’d have to tie up $4,000 in deposits; on
        Premier, you only need the $400 deposit).

Quick Comparison

| Feature               | Basic                                       | Premier                            |
| :-------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------- |
| **Annual Fee**        | Lower (\~$99)                               | Higher (\~$249)                    |
| **Deposit Required**  | 10% of total bid amount                     | Flat $400 (one-time, refundable)   |
| **Simultaneous Bids** | 1 car at a time                             | Multiple cars at once              |
| **Bidding Ceiling**   | Capped by how much you deposit              | Up to $100,000+ daily              |
| **Ideal User**        | Individual buying a personal car or project | Rebuilders, dealerships, exporters |

Summary Recommendation

  - If you plan to buy just one car to fix up or use for parts, stick with
    Basic. Deposit only what you need for that specific vehicle.
  - If you plan to buy multiple cars a year, bid on expensive late-model
    vehicles ($10,000+), or bid on several lots at once to see which one you get
    the best deal on, Premier saves time and prevents you from having large cash
    deposits tied up.
